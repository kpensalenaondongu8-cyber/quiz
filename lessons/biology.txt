1. CELL STRUCTURE & ORGANELLES
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of all living things.
- Mitochondrion: Known as the "powerhouse of the cell." It performs 
  cellular respiration to create ATP (the cell's primary energy currency).
- Prokaryotes: Simple, single-celled organisms (like Bacteria) that 
  lack a defined nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
- Cell Wall: A rigid outer layer made of cellulose found in plant cells 
  that provides structural support. Animal cells lack this completely.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PLANT PROCESSES & PHOTOSYNTHESIS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Plants use specialized tissues and chemical processes to survive and grow:
- Chlorophyll: The green pigment inside plant chloroplasts that absorbs 
  sunlight to drive photosynthesis.
- Transpiration: The process where water moves up from plant roots and 
  evaporates as water vapor out of tiny leaf pores called stomata.
- Xylem vs. Phloem: Xylem tissue transports water and minerals upward 
  from the roots, while Phloem distributes food and sugars downward.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
3. CELL DIVISION & REPRODUCTION
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Cells replicate using two main types of cellular division:
- Mitosis: Used for growth and tissue repair. It results in two identical 
  daughter cells with the same number of chromosomes as the parent cell.
- Meiosis: Used strictly for sexual reproduction. It cuts the chromosome 
  count in half, resulting in four unique haploid gamete cells (sperm/egg).

----------------------------------------------------------------------
4. GENETICS & INHERITANCE
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid): The double-stranded helical molecule 
  carrying the complete genetic blueprint for all living organisms.
- Gregor Mendel: Regarded as the "Father of Modern Genetics" for tracking 
  inherited traits across generations of pea plants in the 1860s.
- Recessive Trait: A genetic trait that is masked by dominant alleles. 
  It will only physically show up if a child inherits it from both parents.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
5. HUMAN ANATOMY & INTERNAL ORGANS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- The Liver: The largest and heaviest internal organ in the human body. 
  It handles critical tasks like detoxification and producing bile.
- The Pancreas: An endocrine organ responsible for producing insulin, 
  which balances and regulates blood glucose levels.
- The Kidneys: Organs containing millions of tiny filtering units called 
  Nephrons that clean blood, remove waste, and produce urine.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
6. CIRCULATORY & DIGESTIVE SYSTEMS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Arteries: High-pressure blood vessels that carry oxygenated blood away 
  from the heart to bodily tissues (Except the pulmonary artery).
- Red Blood Cells (Erythrocytes): Cells packed with an iron-rich protein 
  called hemoglobin that binds to oxygen and carries it around the body.
- Small Intestine: The principal site of nutrient absorption. It uses 
  millions of tiny finger-like projections called villi to pull nutrients 
  directly into the bloodstream.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
7. HORMONES & NERVOUS COORDINATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Adrenaline: The "fight or flight" hormone released by adrenal glands during 
  emergencies. It increases heart rate and spikes energy levels.
- Synapse: The microscopic gap or structural connection between two nerve 
  cells (neurons) where chemical signals cross to pass impulses along.
- Vitamin D: A vital nutrient synthesized naturally by human skin cells 
  when exposed to direct ultraviolet B (UVB) rays from sunlight.
