1. ATOMIC STRUCTURE & PARTICLES
----------------------------------------------------------------------
An atom is the basic building block of chemistry. It consists of three 
main subatomic particles:
- Protons: Positively charged particles located in the central nucleus.
- Neutrons: Neutrally charged (no charge) particles located in the nucleus.
- Electrons: Negatively charged particles that orbit rapidly around the 
  outside of the nucleus.

Properties & Identifiers:
- Isotopes: Atoms of the same element that have the exact same number 
  of protons but different numbers of neutrons (giving them different masses).
- Hydrogen: The atomic number 1 element. It is the lightest and most 
  abundant element in the universe.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE PERIODIC TABLE
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The Periodic Table organizes all known chemical elements.
- Creation: Russian chemist Dmitri Mendeleev created the first widely 
  accepted periodic table in 1869, leaving gaps for undiscovered elements.
- Noble Gases: Located in Group 18 (e.g., Helium, Neon, Argon). They have 
  a full outer electron shell, making them extremely stable and unreactive.
- Chemical Symbols: Some symbols come from Latin roots. For example, 
  Gold is "Au" (from aurum) and Sodium is "Na" (from natrium).

----------------------------------------------------------------------
3. CHEMICAL BONDS & VALENCE
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Atoms bond with each other by interacting with their outer electrons to 
achieve chemical stability.
- Covalent Bond: Formed when two non-metal atoms share electrons.
- Ionic Bond: Formed when atoms transfer electrons to one another, 
  creating charged ions that attract each other.
- Carbon's Role: Organic chemistry is the dedicated study of carbon-based 
  compounds. Carbon is unique because it can form stable covalent bonds 
  with up to four other atoms.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
4. PHYSICAL VS. CHEMICAL CHANGES
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Physical Change: Alters the form or appearance of a substance without 
  creating a new chemical composition.
  Examples: Melting ice, dissolving sugar in water, freezing water.
- Chemical Change: A chemical reaction occurs, creating a completely new 
  substance with new chemical properties.
  Examples: Rusting iron (creates iron oxide), burning wood, souring milk.

Special Phase Transitions:
- Sublimation: The unique process where a solid turns directly into a gas 
  without passing through a liquid state (e.g., dry ice, iodine crystals).

----------------------------------------------------------------------
5. ACIDS, BASES, & THE pH SCALE
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The pH scale measures how acidic or basic a water-based solution is. It 
ranges from 0 to 14.
- Acids: Have a pH below 7. They taste sour and turn blue litmus paper red.
- Neutral: Has a pH of exactly 7 at room temperature (e.g., Pure Water).
- Bases (Alkalies): Have a pH above 7. They feel slippery and turn red 
  litmus paper blue.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
6. REACTION MECHANISMS & LAWS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Exothermic Reaction: A reaction that releases thermal energy (heat) 
  into its surroundings, causing temperatures to rise.
- Endothermic Reaction: A reaction that absorbs heat from its surroundings.
- Catalyst: A substance that speeds up a chemical reaction by lowering 
  the activation energy required, without being consumed or altered itself.
- Law of Conservation of Mass: Dictates that matter cannot be created or 
  destroyed in a closed chemical reaction; mass of reactants equals the 
  mass of products.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
7. EXTRACTIONS & ATMOSPHERIC GASES
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Earth's Atmosphere: Composed of approximately 78% Nitrogen gas ($N_2$) 
  and 21% Oxygen gas ($O_2$), making Nitrogen the most abundant gas.
- Ozone ($O_3$): An allotrope of oxygen consisting of three bound atoms 
  that protects Earth from harmful UV rays.
- Bauxite: The primary sedimentary rock ore used globally to extract and 
  refine Aluminium metal.
