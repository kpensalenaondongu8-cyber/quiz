1. NATIONAL SYMBOLS & IDENTITY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Nigeria's national identity is represented by specific official symbols:

- The National Flag: Designed in 1959 by Michael Taiwo Akinkunmi while 
  he was studying engineering in London. It features two green vertical 
  bands representing Nigeria's rich natural resources and agricultural 
  wealth, and a middle white band standing for peace.

- The Coat of Arms: Features unique structural symbols:
  - The two white horses represent the dignity and pride of Nigeria.
  - The eagle represents strength.
  - The 'Y' shaped silver band represents the confluence of Nigeria's 
    two main rivers: River Niger and River Benue.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
2. HISTORICAL MILESTONES & REPUBLICANISM
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Independence (1960): Nigeria officially gained freedom from British 
  colonial rule on October 1, 1960. Sir Abubakar Tafawa Balewa served 
  as the country's first and only Prime Minister.

- Becoming a Republic (1963): On October 1, 1963, Nigeria became a 
  Federal Republic, completely severing constitutional ties with the 
  British monarchy and replacing the Queen with an indigenous President.

- The National Youth Service Corps (NYSC): Established on May 22, 1973, 
  under General Yakubu Gowon's military regime to rehabilitate, 
  reconstruct, and promote national unity after the Nigerian Civil War.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
3. POLITICAL LEADERSHIP PIONEERS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- First Executive President: Alhaji Shehu Shagari became Nigeria's 
  first democratically elected Executive President in 1979 under the 
  Second Republic framework.

- Geopolitical Structure: Nigeria is administratively divided into 
  36 States, plus the Federal Capital Territory (FCT). These states are 
  grouped into 6 Geopolitical Zones: North-West, North-East, 
  North-Central, South-West, South-East, and South-South.

- Federal Capital Territory: Abuja officially became the administrative 
  capital city of Nigeria on December 12, 1991, taking over the status 
  from the coastal city of Lagos.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
4. REVENUE, CURRENCY, & INSTITUTIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- The Currency (Naira): The Nigerian Naira (₦) was introduced on 
  January 1, 1973, replacing the Nigerian Pound (£) and shifting the 
  country to a decimal currency system.

- Economic Mainstay: The extraction, refining, and export of Crude Oil 
  has been the dominant driver of Nigeria's foreign exchange earnings 
  since the late 1960s.

- INEC: The Independent National Electoral Commission is the official 
  statutory body set up to oversee and conduct federal and state-level 
  elections across the country.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
5. GLOBAL ACHIEVEMENTS & ARTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Wole Soyinka: Won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1986, making him 
  the first Nigerian and first African to win a Nobel Prize in that 
  prestigious field.

- Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala: Made global history when she became the first 
  woman and first African to be appointed Director-General of the 
  World Trade Organization (WTO).

- Amina J. Mohammed: Serves on the global stage as the Deputy 
  Secretary-General of the United Nations, working closely with the 
  UN Secretary-General to drive sustainable development.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
6. GEOGRAPHY, MEDIA, & JUDICIARY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Principal Waterway: River Niger is the principal river of West 
  Africa and the longest river in Nigeria, giving the country its name.

- WNTV: The Western Nigeria Television (WNTV) was launched in October 
  1959 by Chief Obafemi Awolowo, making it the first indigenous 
  television broadcast station in Nigeria and tropical Africa.

- The Supreme Court: Located in Abuja, it stands as the absolute highest 
  court of law in Nigeria. Its judicial determinations are ultimate 
  and final.
