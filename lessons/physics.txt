1. FUNDAMENTAL SI UNITS & FORCES
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Physics relies on standard international (SI) metrics to quantify values.
- Force: Measured in Newtons (N). Force is any action that changes or
  maintains the motion of a physical body.
- Power: Measured in Watts (W). Power equals work done per unit time 
  (1 Watt = 1 Joule per second).
- Resistance: Measured in Ohms (Ω). Quantifies how heavily a material
  impedes electrical currents.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
2. CLASSICAL MECHANICS & NEWTON'S LAWS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Sir Isaac Newton established three fundamental laws governing motion:

- First Law (Law of Inertia): Objects resist changes to their current 
  velocity. A body at rest stays at rest, and a body in motion stays 
  in motion unless acted upon by an external net force.
- Second Law: Acceleration happens when a force acts on a mass.
  Formula: Force = Mass × Acceleration (F = ma)
- Third Law: For every action force, there is an equal and opposite 
  reaction force. Forces always exist in interacting pairs.

⚠️ Earth's Gravity: Free-falling objects near Earth's surface accelerate 
downward at a standard constant rate of approximately 9.8 m/s².

----------------------------------------------------------------------
3. STATES OF MATTER & COMPOSITION
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Solid: Fixed, rigid structure with a definite shape and volume.
- Liquid: Molecules shift dynamically. Conforms to container shapes 
  but maintains a constant, unyielding volume.
- Gas: No fixed shape or volume. Expands to completely fill spaces.
- Density: The concentration of mass packed into a specific volume.
  Formula: Density = Mass / Volume (D = m/V)

----------------------------------------------------------------------
4. ENERGY & THERMODYNAMICS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Energy cannot be created or destroyed; it only changes forms.
- Kinetic Energy: Energy possessed by an object due to its active motion.
- Potential Energy: Stored energy possessed by an object due to its 
  relative height or position inside a gravitational field.

Heat Transfer Mechanisms:
- Conduction: Thermal energy traveling through direct molecular contact.
- Convection: Heat circulating via fluid movements (liquids or gases).
- Radiation: Heat moving as electromagnetic waves without mediums.

⚠️ Absolute Zero: The lowest theoretical temperature limit (0 Kelvin or 
-273.15°C) where all molecular kinetic motion stops entirely.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WAVE PHENOMENA & OPTICS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Waves transmit energy across spatial paths through distinct structures:
- Longitudinal Waves: Particles vibrate parallel to wave direction 
  via compression patterns (e.g., Sound Waves require physical mediums).
- Transverse Waves: Particles vibrate perpendicularly (e.g., Light Waves).
- Frequency: Count of cyclic wave oscillations per second, measured 
  in Hertz (Hz).
- Speed of Light: Light travels through a vacuum at a universal max limit 
  of roughly 300,000,000 meters per second (3 × 10⁸ m/s).

Optics & Lenses:
- Refraction: The bending of light waves when traversing from one transparent 
  medium into another of different structural density (e.g., water glass).
- Concave Lens: Curves inward, forcing incoming light rays to diverge outward.
- Convex Lens: Curves outward, converging light rays toward a focal center.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
6. ELECTRICITY & CIRCUITS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Electricity is defined as a systematic stream of free-moving valence 
electrons passing along a conductive medium path.

Ohm's Law:
Defines the absolute proportional relationship between Voltage (V), 
Current (I), and Resistance (R).
Formula: Voltage = Current × Resistance (V = IR)

Circuit Tools:
- Ammeter: Connected strictly in series to track active current (Amperes).
- Voltmeter: Connected in parallel across components to measure potential 
  differences (Volts).
- Friction: An electrostatic or mechanical force working explicitly to 
  impede contact paths sliding over each other.
